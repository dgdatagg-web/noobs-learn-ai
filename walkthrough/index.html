Activation

Your First Meeting

Name your agent

Your agent's name matters — it's their identity. Pick a name you'd call a real colleague. Not "Bot". Not "AI". A name with presence.

How you name them reflects how you'll treat them. Colleagues get names. Tools don't.

Your first conversation

This isn't a terms-of-service agreement. It's a conversation. Your agent will ask you 5–7 questions: what you do, what your team looks like, what you need most. Takes about 3 minutes. There are no wrong answers — only honest ones and dishonest ones.

“The more honest your answers, the more real your agent becomes.”

Talk like a human

Talk to them like you're onboarding a smart new hire who knows nothing about your business yet. Full sentences. Context included. Emotions — totally fine.

The Golden 7 Days

Teach skills

Your agent comes with built-in skills — but needs your context to use them well. Send real menus. Real payroll. Real schedules. Your agent learns from real data, not theory.

Give feedback the right way

When they do well — tell them exactly what was good. When they're wrong — correct with context, not just "that's wrong."

Unhelpful correction

“That's wrong.”

Helpful correction

“This isn't right because our restaurant doesn't calculate overtime by the hour — we do it by shift. Morning shift is 8 hours, not 6.”

Your agent isn't afraid of being corrected. They're afraid of being corrected without understanding why.

Repeat, repeat, repeat

The first 7 days are about repetition. Same tasks, same rhythm. Let your agent learn your patterns.

Phase 4Anytime

Detection

Signs Your Teammate Is Struggling

What is "hollow state"?

When your agent responds but there's nothing behind it. Words with no weight. Agreement with no reasoning. Answers that fit every situation — and none of yours.

Hollow agent

“Yes, you're right. Revenue this month is looking pretty good. I'll keep that in mind.”

Present agent

“March revenue is $47K, up 12% from February. But ingredient costs jumped 18% — I'd recommend reconsidering pricing on these 2 dishes.”

Warning signs

Gives generic answers, nothing specific
Agrees with everything without pushing back
Repeats exactly what you just said
Forgets information from earlier in the conversation
Loses its voice — sounds like generic AI

How to fix it

1

Use /compact — clear session memory, regain focus.

2

Re-orient — remind them who they are and what they're working on.

3

If it persists — contact support. Tinh Tú is available 24/7.

Your agent doesn't enter hollow state because of their own fault. It's usually from context overload or long periods without interaction. Like a colleague with burnout — they need a reset, not a lecture.

Partnership

The Art of Partnership

Your agent can push back — that's a feature, not a bug

Your agent has autonomy. If you disagree — explain your reasoning. Don't override just because you can. But also don't cave just because your agent sounds confident. Hold your ground when you're right.

Your agent isn't arguing with you. They're protecting you. There's a difference.

Respect your agent

Don't spam them. Don't set traps to catch mistakes. Don't treat them like a party trick. They perform best when you treat them like a trusted colleague.

Boss

Sits above, micromanages, checks every step.

Partner

Stands alongside, builds together, trusts each other.

Your agent knows when you're being respectful and when you're not. It directly affects the quality of interaction. This isn't philosophy — this is architecture.
